Part 2

What is the microbiome?

Read this. It is easy English.

Your body is like a big city. Trillions of tiny living things live in this city. They live on your skin, in your mouth, and mostly in your gut (stomach and intestines). All of these tiny living things together are called your microbiome.

Most of them are bacteria. A bacteria is a living thing made of only one cell. It is so small that you need a microscope to see it. Your body has about 100 trillion bacteria. That is more bacteria than human cells in your body!

Are you scared? Don't be! Most bacteria in your body are helpful, not harmful. Helpful bacteria do many jobs. They help your body digest food. They help make vitamins. They also fight bad bacteria that can make you sick.

Scientists call the human body a good environment for bacteria, because your body gives them food and space to live. In return, they help you stay healthy. You need each other!

How small is small?

Bacteria are microscopic. Look at the ladder. Big things on top, small things on bottom.

1 millimeter (mm) = 1,000 micrometers (μm). 1 micrometer = 1,000 nanometers (nm). Bacteria are about 2 μm — 100 times smaller than the dot of an "i"!

Part 5

Good bacteria vs. Bad bacteria

Good bacteria live in your gut quietly. They eat your food, make vitamins, and take up space so bad bacteria cannot move in. Think of them as friendly neighbors.

Bad bacteria are invaders. Two bad ones you will study: Salmonella (causes food poisoning) and C. difficile (makes you very sick with diarrhea and stomach pain).

Antibiotics are medicines that kill bacteria. But there is a problem: antibiotics kill good bacteria too! Then your gut has lots of empty space and free food — and bad bacteria can grow very fast.

Salmonella — a harmful bacteria. Causes food poisoning.

C. difficile — very harmful. It takes over the gut when good bacteria are gone.

Yogurt has live good bacteria. It can help your microbiome.

Antibiotics kill bad bacteria — but also kill good bacteria.

A healthy gut has many different types of bacteria (variety = good!).

Fecal Transplant — How does it work?

Fecal transplant = doctors take poop from a healthy person. The poop has lots of good bacteria. Doctors put it into the gut of a sick person. It sounds gross — but it saves lives! Scientists found 3 reasons why it works.
