# NYC / Brooklyn Grade 4 Math Curriculum Research

## Scope Note
Brooklyn is part of New York City Public Schools (NYC DOE), which follows the **New York State P-12 Next Generation Mathematics Learning Standards** (NGLS). NYC elementary schools have historically used the **EngageNY/Eureka Math** curriculum ("A Story of Units"); the materials are now archived by NYSED but remain widely used. The standards below are the official NYS codes a Brooklyn Grade 4 student is expected to master.

---

## 1. Operations & Algebraic Thinking (NY-4.OA)

| Standard | Skill / Expectation | Example Problem Types |
|----------|---------------------|------------------------|
| **NY-4.OA.1** | Interpret multiplication equations as comparisons; represent verbal multiplicative comparisons as equations. | "35 = 5 × 7 means 35 is 5 times as many as 7." Write "Four times as many as eight is thirty-two" as 4 × 8 = 32. |
| **NY-4.OA.2** | Multiply or divide to solve word problems involving multiplicative comparison; distinguish multiplicative from additive comparison. | "Carter has 9 comic books. Ben has 3 times as many. How many does Ben have?" (2024 state test, Q1). |
| **NY-4.OA.3a/3b** | Solve multi-step word problems using the four operations; represent with equations/expressions using a letter for the unknown; assess reasonableness with mental math/estimation/rounding. | "80 students go to the zoo. Bus = $3 each, admission = $2 each. What is the total cost?" (2024, Q32). Remainders must be interpreted in context. |
| **NY-4.OA.4** | Find all factor pairs for whole numbers 1-100; recognize multiples; determine prime or composite. | "List all the factors of 21." (2024, Q37). Identify whether 47 is prime or composite. |
| **NY-4.OA.5** | Generate number or shape patterns from a given rule; identify and informally explain features not explicit in the rule. | Given "Add 3" starting at 1, observe and explain why terms alternate odd/even. |

**Instructional note:** Order of operations is a Grade 5 expectation (NY-5.OA.1); multi-step Grade 4 problems need not be represented by a single expression.

## 2. Number & Operations in Base Ten (NY-4.NBT)

All Grade 4 work is limited to **whole numbers ≤ 1,000,000**.

| Standard | Skill / Expectation | Example Problem Types |
|----------|---------------------|------------------------|
| **NY-4.NBT.1** | Recognize a digit in one place represents 10 times what it represents in the place to its right. | "How many hundreds are in 1,000?" (2024, Q23). 70 × 10 = 700; 700 ÷ 70 = 10. |
| **NY-4.NBT.2a/2b** | Read/write multi-digit whole numbers in base-ten numerals, number names, and expanded form; compare using >, =, <. | Compare 4,699 and 4,780 using place value; write 50,327 = 50,000 + 300 + 20 + 7. (2024, Q40). |
| **NY-4.NBT.3** | Round multi-digit whole numbers to any place. | "Which number rounds to 17,000 when rounded to the nearest thousand?" (2024, Q12). |
| **NY-4.NBT.4** | Fluently add and subtract multi-digit whole numbers using a standard algorithm. | Multi-digit addition/subtraction with regrouping up to 1,000,000. |
| **NY-4.NBT.5** | Multiply up to 4-digit by 1-digit and two 2-digit numbers; use place value, properties of operations; illustrate with equations, rectangular arrays, area models. | "What is the product of 3 and 2,470?" (2024, Q29). Area-model completion for 2-digit × 2-digit. (2024, Q34). |
| **NY-4.NBT.6** | Find whole-number quotients and remainders with up to 4-digit dividends and 1-digit divisors; use place value, properties, and multiplication/division relationship. | "A team earned $170; soccer balls cost $9 each. What is the greatest number they can buy?" (2024, Q39). |

**Instructional note:** Formal algorithms for multiplication and division are not required until Grades 5 and 6 respectively; Grade 4 emphasizes strategies and models.

## 3. Number & Operations—Fractions (NY-4.NF)

All Grade 4 fraction expectations are limited to denominators **2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, and 100**.

| Standard | Skill / Expectation | Example Problem Types |
|----------|---------------------|------------------------|
| **NY-4.NF.1** | Explain fraction equivalence using visual models; recognize and generate equivalent fractions. | Show 1/2 = 2/4 with a tape diagram; find equivalent fractions. (2024, Q2). |
| **NY-4.NF.2** | Compare fractions with different numerators/denominators; recognize comparisons require the same whole; justify with common denominators, numerators, or benchmarks (e.g., 1/2). | "Which number sentence shows a correct comparison?" (2024, Q33). |
| **NY-4.NF.3a** | Understand addition/subtraction of fractions as joining/separating parts of the same whole. | "Which fraction can be added to ___ to equal 1 whole?" (2024, Q11). |
| **NY-4.NF.3b** | Decompose a fraction into a sum of like-denominator fractions in more than one way; justify with visual models. | Write 3/8 = 1/8 + 1/8 + 1/8 = 1/8 + 2/8. (2024, Q35). |
| **NY-4.NF.3c** | Add and subtract mixed numbers with like denominators. | 2 1/8 + 1 3/8; replace mixed numbers with equivalent fractions or use properties. (2024, Q22). |
| **NY-4.NF.3d** | Solve word problems involving addition/subtraction of like-denominator fractions. | Pizza or rope-length word problems with same-denominator fractions. |
| **NY-4.NF.4** | Multiply a whole number by a fraction (repeated addition of unit fractions). | "If each person eats 3/8 lb of roast beef and 5 people attend, how many pounds are needed?" (NYSED example). (2024, Q15, Q42). |
| **NY-4.NF.5** | Express a fraction with denominator 10 as an equivalent fraction with denominator 100; add fractions with denominators 10 and 100. | 3/10 + 4/100 = 30/100 + 4/100 = 34/100. |
| **NY-4.NF.6** | Use decimal notation for fractions with denominators 10 or 100. | Rewrite 0.62 as 62/100; locate 0.62 on a number line; describe 0.62 meters. |
| **NY-4.NF.7** | Compare two decimals to hundredths by reasoning about size; recognize same-whole requirement; justify with visual models. | Compare 0.45 and 0.5 using a hundredths grid. |

**Instructional note:** NY-4.NF.4 focuses on **whole number × fraction** (e.g., 4 × 1/3). Fraction × whole number (e.g., 1/3 × 4) is introduced in Grade 5 (NY-5.NF.4a).

## 4. Measurement & Data (NY-4.MD)

| Standard | Skill / Expectation | Example Problem Types |
|----------|---------------------|------------------------|
| **NY-4.MD.1** | Know relative sizes and conversion factors for **ft./in.; km/m/cm; hr./min./sec.**; convert larger to smaller units within a single system; record equivalents in a table. | Convert 4 ft. to 48 in.; 1 km = 1,000 m; generate a ft./in. conversion table. Other single-system conversions use a given factor. |
| **NY-4.MD.2a/2b** | Solve four-operation word problems involving distances, time, liquid volumes, masses, and money with fractions/decimals; express larger units as smaller units; represent quantities on scaled number-line diagrams. | Measurement word problems with like-denominator fractions or decimals; money/metric conversion problems. |
| **NY-4.MD.3** | Apply area and perimeter formulas for rectangles in real-world and mathematical problems. | "The perimeter of a square floor is 120 ft. What is the length of each side?" (2024, Q30). Find width given area and length. |
| **NY-4.MD.4** | Make a line plot to display data in fractions of a unit (1/2, 1/4, 1/8); solve addition/subtraction problems using the line plot. | Given a line plot of book-report hours, find the total time over 4 days. (2024, Q41). |
| **NY-4.MD.5a/5b** | Understand angle measurement: angle as a fraction of a circle (1/360 = 1°); angle measure is additive. | Explain that an angle turning through n one-degree angles measures n degrees. |
| **NY-4.MD.6** | Measure angles in whole-number degrees using a protractor; sketch angles of specified measure. | "What is the measure, in degrees, of the angle shown?" (2024, Q36). |
| **NY-4.MD.7** | Recognize angle measure as additive; solve addition/subtraction problems to find unknown angles on a diagram. | "Ray KM divides straight angle JKL into two parts. Which equation finds the measure of angle JKM?" (2024, Q7). |

## 5. Geometry (NY-4.G)

| Standard | Skill / Expectation | Example Problem Types |
|----------|---------------------|------------------------|
| **NY-4.G.1** | Draw and identify points, lines, line segments, rays, angles (right, acute, obtuse), and perpendicular/parallel lines; identify these in 2-D figures. | "Which statement about the diagram is most likely true?" (parallel/perpendicular lines) (2024, Q10, Q43). |
| **NY-4.G.2a** | Identify and name triangles based on angle size (right, obtuse, acute). | "A square is divided into two equal triangles. What type of triangles are created?" (2024, Q38). |
| **NY-4.G.2b/2c** | Identify quadrilaterals with two pairs of parallel sides as parallelograms; identify quadrilaterals with four right angles as rectangles. | "Which figure appears to be a rectangle?" (2024, Q26). |
| **NY-4.G.3** | Recognize a line of symmetry; identify line-symmetric figures; draw lines of symmetry. | "How many lines of symmetry does the figure have?" (2024, Q31). |

**Instructional note:** The full hierarchy of 2-D figures/quadrilateral family is a Grade 5 expectation (NY-5.G.3/4).

## Typical EngageNY/Eureka Math Module Sequence

NYC schools that use EngageNY/Eureka Math organize Grade 4 into 7 modules (~153 instructional days):

1. **Module 1:** Place Value, Rounding, and Algorithms for Addition and Subtraction
2. **Module 2:** Multi-Step Word Problems and Equations (focus on metric conversions)
3. **Module 3:** Multi-Digit Multiplication and Division
4. **Module 4:** Angle Measure and Plane Figures
5. **Module 5:** Fraction Equivalence, Ordering, and Operations
6. **Module 6:** Decimal Fractions
7. **Module 7:** Exploring Measurement with Multiplication (U.S. customary conversions)

---

## Assessment Context

- **NYS Grades 3-8 Math Test:** Administered in spring (typically April/May). Grade 4 students answer multiple-choice and constructed-response (1-, 2-, and 3-credit) questions aligned to the NGLS standards above.
- **Tools:** Students may use a ruler and protractor where helpful.
