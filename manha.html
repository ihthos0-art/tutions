The Lost Library Book

Manha had borrowed a library book called Ocean Animals. She was so excited that she read three chapters the very first night. The book had glossy pictures of dolphins, giant whales, and deep-sea fish that glowed in the dark. She promised herself she would take very good care of it because it belonged to everyone at the library, not just her.

A week passed, and when Manha went to put the book back on her shelf, it was not there. She searched under her bed, in her backpack, and inside every drawer in her room. Her stomach began to feel tight and anxious. The book was due back in two days, and she had no idea where it had gone.

Manha told her mother what happened. Her mother said calmly, “Think carefully about the last place you had it.” Manha remembered reading it during lunch at school. The next morning, she went straight to her teacher and explained everything honestly. Her teacher walked her to the cafeteria lost and found. And there it was — Ocean Animals, sitting right on top of the pile, safe and unharmed.

Manha felt her whole body relax the moment she held the book again. She returned it to the library on time and even apologized to the librarian for almost losing it. The librarian smiled and said, “The best thing you did was tell the truth and keep looking.” Manha walked home feeling lighter than she had in days. She learned that being responsible did not just mean being careful — it also meant being honest when something went wrong.

The Science Fair

Javier had been working on his science fair project for three whole weeks. He was building a model volcano out of clay, and he had practiced making it erupt with baking soda and vinegar at least six times at home. Every eruption worked perfectly — a white foamy burst that shot straight up over the top of the cone. He was certain he had a strong chance at first place.

The night before the fair, disaster struck. While Javier was carrying the volcano to the car, he stumbled on the front step. The model hit the ground and split right down the middle. Javier stared at the two pieces in the driveway and felt tears pressing behind his eyes. Three weeks of careful work, ruined in one second. He wanted to just go to bed and forget the whole thing had ever happened.

His father sat down beside him on the driveway. “We can fix this,” he said quietly. Together they worked past midnight, pressing the clay back together and smoothing every crack. His father found brown paint in the garage and covered the damage. It was not as perfect as before, but it looked like a rough, real volcano — the kind you might see a photograph of in a science book.

At the science fair the next morning, Javier did not win first place. But when the eruption went off in a huge foamy burst, the entire gym cheered. His teacher gave him a special ribbon that said “Most Determined.” Javier taped it to his bedroom door that evening and decided it was his favorite prize of all — not because he had won, but because he had not given up when everything felt impossible.

Manha and the Ladybug Kingdom

One afternoon, Manha was playing in her backyard when she spotted something glowing behind the rosebush. It was a tiny pink door, no taller than her knee, with a golden ladybug-shaped handle. Manha took a deep breath and turned the handle. With a pop and a shimmer of pink sparkles, she tumbled through the door and landed softly on a cloud of cotton-candy fluff. She looked around and gasped. Everything was pink — the sky, the trees, the river, and even the little houses made of rose petals. A tiny sign in front of her said: Welcome to Ladybug Kingdom.

Before Manha could take another step, a ladybug the size of her thumb zoomed down from a pink cloud. She had eight sparkly spots and tiny silver wings. “My name is Luna,” the ladybug said, buzzing with worry. “You came just in time! The Glow Berry — the magical fruit that keeps all our colors bright — has gone missing from the Rainbow Tower. Without it, Ladybug Kingdom is turning grey!” Manha looked toward the edge of the kingdom and saw it was true. The pink was fading away like a watercolor left in the rain. She straightened her shoulders. “I’ll help you find it,” she said. Luna clapped all six of her tiny legs together.

Manha and Luna searched the Petal Forest, the Honey River, and the Mushroom Library. At last, near the base of the Rainbow Tower, Manha noticed something round and glowing, buried under a pile of leaves. It was the Glow Berry! A clumsy little caterpillar had knocked it off the shelf by accident and hadn’t noticed. Manha picked it up gently and carried it back to its place at the very top of the tower. In an instant, pink flooded back into every corner of Ladybug Kingdom. Luna hugged Manha’s finger and gave her a tiny glowing ladybug badge as a thank-you gift. When Manha stepped back through the little pink door and into her own backyard, she was smiling so wide her cheeks hurt. She put the badge on her jacket and wore it every single day after that.

■ Where Did They Live?

The ancient Egyptians lived in Egypt, in the continent of Africa.

They lived near the Nile River. The rest of the land was hot, dry desert.

The Nile River gave them water, food, and rich soil to grow plants.

■■ What Was the Climate Like?

Egypt was hot and dry. It did not rain much.

Most of Egypt was desert. It could get very, very hot.

Near the Nile River, the land was green and good for farming.

■ Why Was the Nile River Important?

The Nile River helped Egyptians survive. Here is how:

  • It gave them water to drink and cook with.
  • They used it to water their crops (called irrigation).
  • Every year it flooded and left behind rich soil to grow food.
  • They used it to travel and move things by boat.
  • They caught fish from it for food.
  • Plants like papyrus grew along it and were used to make paper.
■ Foods They Ate
  • Bread — the most important food. Made from wheat.
  • Beer — made from grain. Almost everyone drank it.
  • Fish — caught from the Nile River.
  • Vegetables — onions, garlic, and lentils.
  • Fruit — figs, dates, and grapes.
  • Meat — rich people ate beef and duck. Poor people ate less meat.

■■ What Were Pyramids Used For?

Pyramids were burial places for pharaohs (the kings of Egypt).

Egyptians believed in life after death. They wanted to keep the pharaoh’s body safe.

  • The mummy of the pharaoh was kept inside.
  • Treasures and food were stored inside for the afterlife.
  • The pyramid shape pointed up to the sky and the gods.
  • Secret rooms helped keep robbers away.
■■ Religion and Mummification

Why did pharaohs want big pyramids?

Pharaohs thought they were gods. They wanted a big, strong pyramid to be their home after death. A big pyramid showed how powerful they were.

How did embalmers dry out the body?

  • First, they took out the organs (like the stomach and lungs).
  • They packed the body with natron — a salty powder that soaks up water.
  • The body was left for 40 days to dry out.
  • Then they wrapped it in cloth called linen. Now it was a mummy!

What is a sarcophagus? What goes inside?

A sarcophagus is a big, decorated stone coffin. It held the mummy safe.

Inside you would find:

  • The mummy of the pharaoh.
  • Jars called canopic jars that held the organs.
  • Jewelry, food, and treasures for the afterlife.
  • A scroll with special spells to help the soul.

■ Egypt — Country Research Sheet
This part is for the new worksheet pages about Egypt today.

Basic Facts:

Continent:Africa
Population:About 105 million people
Capital City:Cairo
Language:Arabic

Food in Egypt:

  • Bread (Aish) — flat bread eaten at every meal.
  • Koshari — rice, lentils, and pasta mixed together.
  • Falafel — small fried balls made from beans.
  • Fava beans (Ful) — a popular breakfast food.
  • Dates and figs — sweet fruits grown in Egypt.

The Flag:

The Egyptian flag has 3 stripes: Red, White, and Black. In the middle there is a golden eagle. The eagle is called the Eagle of Saladin.

Famous Landmarks:

  • The Great Pyramid of Giza — one of the oldest and biggest buildings in the world.
  • The Sphinx — a giant statue with a lion body and human head.
  • The Valley of the Kings — a place with many royal tombs.
  • Karnak Temple — a very large ancient temple.

Holidays and Traditions:

  • Sham el-Nessim — a spring holiday. Families go outside and share food.
  • Eid al-Fitr — a big holiday after a month of fasting. Families eat together and give gifts.
  • Ramadan — a holy month. People fast during the day and eat together at night.

Famous People:

  • Cleopatra — a powerful queen of ancient Egypt.
  • Ramesses II — a very strong pharaoh who built many temples.
  • King Tutankhamun (King Tut) — a young pharaoh. His tomb was found in 1922.
  • Mohamed Salah — a famous Egyptian soccer player today.

Climate:

Egypt is hot and dry. Most of Egypt is desert. It does not rain much. Summers are very hot. Winters are warm and cool at night.

Fun Facts:

  • Egyptians invented the 365-day calendar — the same one we use today!
  • Cats were special in ancient Egypt. People treated them like sacred animals.
  • Egyptians wrote using pictures called hieroglyphics.
  • The Great Pyramid was the tallest building in the world for over 3,000 years.
  • Both men and women in ancient Egypt wore eye makeup.
