The Science Fair

Every year, Lincoln Middle School held a science fair in the gym. Students from every grade set up poster boards, models, and experiments on long folding tables. The gym smelled like glue and markers, and the sound of excited voices bounced off the walls.

This year, Keisha decided to enter for the first time. She had spent three weeks researching how plants grow in different kinds of soil. She filled four small pots with sand, clay, garden soil, and compost. Then she planted bean seeds in each one and watered them exactly the same amount every day. She measured their height with a ruler and recorded everything in a notebook.

On the day of the fair, Keisha stood nervously beside her display. A judge walked over and asked her why she chose this project. Keisha took a deep breath and explained that her grandmother had trouble growing tomatoes in sandy soil, and she wanted to find out why. The judge nodded and wrote something on his clipboard. Keisha did not win first place. But when she got home, her grandmother hugged her and said, "You already won something better. You kept asking questions until you found answers."

Question 1 — Central Idea

What is the most important idea in this passage? Write it in one sentence.

Question 2 — Supporting Detail

Find one detail from the passage that shows Keisha worked hard. Use words from the text.

Question 3 — Make a Connection

Have you ever worked hard on something even though you did not win? Write one sentence about it.
