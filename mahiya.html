The Yellow Umbrella

Lily got a new yellow umbrella for her birthday. It was bright like the sun and had little white flowers on it. Lily could not wait to use it. But all week long, the sky was blue and sunny. There was not a single cloud to be seen anywhere.

On Saturday morning, Lily woke up to the sound of rain tapping softly on her window. She jumped out of bed, grabbed her yellow umbrella, and ran outside. She walked down the sidewalk and listened to the rain tap-tap-tap on top of the umbrella. She jumped in every puddle she could find. When she came back inside, she was soaking wet from head to toe, but she was laughing and smiling. “I love rainy days!” she told her mom.

Mahiya and the Word Sprite

Mahiya sat at the kitchen table with her arms crossed tight and the grumpiest frown on her face. Her mom had left a reading worksheet right next to her cereal bowl, and Mahiya did not want to touch it. “I hate reading,” she muttered, pushing the paper away with one finger. “It is so boring and it takes forever.” She slumped so far down in her chair that her chin was almost touching the table. Just then, something small and glowing popped right out from between the pages of her book. It was a tiny creature no bigger than a thumb, with wings made of folded paper and eyes like little blue stars. “Hello, Mahiya,” it said in a voice like a soft whisper. “My name is Pip. I am a Word Sprite, and I live inside stories.” Mahiya blinked. She sat up straight. “You are not real,” she said. Pip did a little spin in the air and laughed a sound like rustling pages. “Read just one sentence,” Pip said, “and I will show you something amazing.”

Mahiya looked down at the worksheet and read the first sentence out loud, slowly. The moment the last word left her lips, the kitchen table disappeared. She was standing in the middle of a glittering forest where the trees had leaves made of letters and the river was full of shining words flowing like water. Pip flew around her head, laughing. “This is what is inside every story,” Pip said. “You just have to open the door.” Mahiya reached out and touched a leaf. It crinkled like paper and smelled like a brand new book. She wanted to see more. She ran along the river reading the words as they floated past, her grumpy frown completely gone. Then, just as quickly as it had appeared, the forest vanished and she was back at the kitchen table. The worksheet was still in front of her. Mahiya picked up her pencil without saying a single word. She did not push the paper away this time. She leaned in close and started to read, because now she knew that somewhere inside every story, Pip was waiting for her.
