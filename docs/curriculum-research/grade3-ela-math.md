# NYC / Brooklyn Grade 3 Curriculum: ELA & Math

**Scope note:** Brooklyn public schools are part of the New York City Public Schools (NYCPS) system. There is no separate "Brooklyn Department of Education." Brooklyn elementary schools follow the same citywide policies and the **New York State Next Generation Learning Standards** used by all NYC schools. Grade 3 is the first year students take the NY State ELA and Math exams (grades 3–8).

---

## 1. Grade 3 English Language Arts (ELA)

NYC uses the **New York State Next Generation English Language Arts Learning Standards**. In Grade 3 the standards are organized as **Reading (3R), Reading Foundations (3RF), Writing (3W), Speaking & Listening (3SL), and Language (3L)**. Grammar/punctuation/spelling conventions for Grades 3–5 are banded; the full list below is the target by the end of 5th grade, with Grade 3 beginning focused work.

### Reading: Literary & Informational Text (3R)

| Code | Standard (summary) |
|------|--------------------|
| **3R1** | Develop and answer questions; locate relevant, specific details to support an answer or inference. (RI & RL) |
| **3R2** | Determine theme/central idea; explain how it is supported by key details; summarize portions of a text. (RI & RL) |
| **3R3** | **Literary:** Describe character traits, motivations, or feelings using specific text details. **Informational:** Describe the relationship among events, ideas, concepts, or steps using time/sequence/cause-and-effect language. |
| **3R4** | Determine the meaning of words, phrases, figurative language, and academic/content-specific words. (RI & RL) |
| **3R5** | **Literary:** Identify parts of stories, dramas, poems using terms such as chapter, scene, stanza. **Informational:** Identify and use text features to build comprehension. |
| **3R6** | Discuss how the reader’s point of view may differ from that of the author, narrator, or characters. (RI & RL) |
| **3R7** | Explain how specific illustrations or text features contribute to what the words convey (mood, character/setting emphasis, where/when/why/how events occur). (RI & RL) |
| **3R8** | Explain how claims in a text are supported by relevant reasons and evidence. (RI & RL) |
| **3R9** | Recognize genres and make connections to other texts, ideas, cultural perspectives, eras, personal events, and situations. (RI & RL) |

### Language: Grammar, Vocabulary, Conventions (3L)

Core grammar and mechanics are taught across the **Grades 3–5 band**; the full list is the end-of-5th-grade target.

| Code | Standard |
|------|----------|
| **3L3** | Recognize differences between spoken conversational and academic English; select conversational or academic forms as appropriate. |
| **3L3a** | Choose words and phrases for effect. |
| **3L3b** | Recognize and observe differences between spoken and written standard English. |
| **3L4** | Determine or clarify the meaning of unknown/multiple-meaning words and phrases using a range of strategies. |
| **3L4a** | Use sentence-level context clues. |
| **3L4b** | Determine meaning of a new word when a known affix is added (e.g., agreeable/disagreeable). |
| **3L4c** | Use a known root word as a clue (e.g., company, companion). |
| **3L4d** | Use glossaries or beginning dictionaries to determine/clarify precise meanings. |
| **3L5** | Demonstrate understanding of word relationships and nuances. |
| **3L5a** | Distinguish literal and nonliteral meanings in context. |
| **3L5b** | Use words for identification and description, connecting words to their use. |
| **3L5c** | Distinguish shades of meaning among related words (knew, believed, suspected, etc.). |
| **3L6** | Acquire and accurately use conversational, general academic, and content-specific words/phrases, including those that signal spatial/temporal relationships. |

**Core Conventions of Academic English — Grades 3–5 (3-5L1):**
- Produce simple, compound, and complex sentences.
- Explain functions of nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, conjunctions, prepositions, interjections.
- Use relative pronouns (*who, whose, whom, which, that*) and relative adverbs (*where, when, why*).
- Form/use regular and irregular plural nouns; use abstract nouns; form/use regular and irregular verbs.
- Form/use simple, progressive, and perfect verb tenses; correct inappropriate shifts in tense.
- Ensure subject–verb and pronoun–antecedent agreement.
- Use coordinating and subordinating conjunctions; identify prepositional phrases.
- Produce complete sentences; recognize/correct fragments and run-ons.
- Correctly use frequently confused words (e.g., *to/too/two*, *there/their*).

**Core Punctuation & Spelling — Grades 3–5 (3-5L2):**
- Capitalize appropriate words in titles; use correct capitalization.
- Use commas in addresses and before coordinating conjunctions in compound sentences.
- Use commas and quotation marks in dialogue/direct speech.
- Use commas to separate introductory elements and items in a series.
- Form and use possessives; use conventional spelling for high-frequency/studied words and suffixes.
- Apply spelling patterns, rules, and generalizations.
- Use quotation marks or italics for titles of works.

### Key Grade 3 ELA Topics / Themes (EngageNY/NYC Reads context)

Typical EngageNY Grade 3 ELA modules (still used by many NYC schools; NYC Reads now requires a DOE-approved structured-literacy curriculum):

1. **Becoming a Close Reader and Writing to Learn** — how people around the world access books; geography and literacy access.
2. **Researching to Build Knowledge and Teach Others** — adaptations and the wide world of frogs (or alternative module).
3. **Connecting Literary and Informational Texts to Study Culture** — Japan then and now.
4. **A Study of Peter Pan** — narrative structure, character, opinion writing.
5. **Wolves: Fact and Fiction** — comparing fictional wolves with real wolves through research.
6. **The Role of Freshwater around the World** — building knowledge through research; PSAs on water conservation.

## 2. Grade 3 Mathematics

NYC uses the **New York State Next Generation Mathematics Learning Standards**. Grade 3 math is organized into five domains: **Operations & Algebraic Thinking (OA), Number & Operations in Base Ten (NBT), Number & Operations—Fractions (NF), Measurement & Data (MD), and Geometry (G)**.

### Operations and Algebraic Thinking (NY-3.OA)

| Code | Standard |
|------|----------|
| **NY-3.OA.1** | Interpret products of whole numbers (e.g., 5 × 7 as 5 groups of 7 objects each). |
| **NY-3.OA.2** | Interpret whole-number quotients (e.g., 56 ÷ 8 as objects in equal shares or number of equal shares). |
| **NY-3.OA.3** | Use multiplication and division within 100 to solve word problems involving equal groups, arrays, and measurement quantities. |
| **NY-3.OA.4** | Determine the unknown whole number in a multiplication or division equation. |
| **NY-3.OA.5** | Apply properties of operations as strategies to multiply and divide (commutative, associative, distributive). |
| **NY-3.OA.6** | Understand division as an unknown-factor problem. |
| **NY-3.OA.7a** | Fluently solve single-digit multiplication and related divisions using strategies or properties. |
| **NY-3.OA.7b** | Know from memory all products of two one-digit numbers. |
| **NY-3.OA.8** | Solve two-step word problems posed with whole numbers using the four operations. |
| **NY-3.OA.8a** | Represent problems with equations/expressions using a letter for the unknown quantity. |
| **NY-3.OA.8b** | Assess reasonableness using mental computation and estimation, including rounding. |
| **NY-3.OA.9** | Identify and extend arithmetic patterns (including patterns in addition and multiplication tables). |

### Number and Operations in Base Ten (NY-3.NBT)

| Code | Standard |
|------|----------|
| **NY-3.NBT.1** | Use place value understanding to round whole numbers to the nearest 10 or 100. |
| **NY-3.NBT.2** | Fluently add and subtract within 1,000 using strategies/algorithms based on place value, properties, and the relationship between addition and subtraction. |
| **NY-3.NBT.3** | Multiply one-digit whole numbers by multiples of 10 in the range 10–90 (e.g., 9 × 80, 5 × 60). |
| **NY-3.NBT.4a** | Understand that digits of a four-digit number represent thousands, hundreds, tens, and ones. |
| **NY-3.NBT.4b** | Read and write four-digit numbers using base-ten numerals, number names, and expanded form. |

### Number and Operations—Fractions (NY-3.NF)

Fractions in Grade 3 are limited to denominators **2, 3, 4, 6, 8**.

| Code | Standard |
|------|----------|
| **NY-3.NF.1** | Understand a unit fraction `1/b` as one part when a whole is partitioned into `b` equal parts; understand `a/b` as `a` parts of size `1/b`. |
| **NY-3.NF.2** | Understand a fraction as a number on the number line; represent fractions on a number line. |
| **NY-3.NF.2a** | Represent `1/b` on a number line by partitioning the interval from 0 to 1 into `b` equal parts. |
| **NY-3.NF.2b** | Represent `a/b` by marking off `a` lengths of `1/b` from 0. |
| **NY-3.NF.3** | Explain equivalence of fractions and compare fractions by reasoning about their size. |
| **NY-3.NF.3a** | Understand two fractions are equivalent if they are the same size or same point on a number line. |
| **NY-3.NF.3b** | Recognize and generate equivalent fractions; explain why (e.g., using a visual model). |
| **NY-3.NF.3c** | Express whole numbers as fractions and recognize fractions equivalent to whole numbers. |
| **NY-3.NF.3d** | Compare two fractions with the same numerator or same denominator; record with `>`, `=`, `<` and justify. |

### Measurement and Data (NY-3.MD)

| Code | Standard |
|------|----------|
| **NY-3.MD.1** | Tell/write time to the nearest minute; measure time intervals in minutes; solve one-step addition/subtraction word problems with time intervals. |
| **NY-3.MD.2a** | Measure and estimate liquid volumes and masses using grams, kilograms, and liters. |
| **NY-3.MD.2b** | Add, subtract, multiply, or divide to solve one-step word problems involving masses or liquid volumes in the same units. |
| **NY-3.MD.3** | Draw scaled picture graphs and scaled bar graphs; solve one- and two-step “how many more/less” problems using data. |
| **NY-3.MD.4** | Generate measurement data by measuring lengths with rulers marked halves/fourths of an inch; make a line plot marked with whole numbers, halves, or quarters. |
| **NY-3.MD.5** | Recognize area as an attribute of plane figures and understand concepts of area measurement. |
| **NY-3.MD.5a** | Recognize a unit square has “one square unit” of area and can measure area. |
| **NY-3.MD.5b** | Recognize a plane figure covered by `n` unit squares has an area of `n` square units. |
| **NY-3.MD.6** | Measure areas by counting unit squares (cm², m², in², ft², improvised units). |
| **NY-3.MD.7** | Relate area to multiplication and addition. |
| **NY-3.MD.7a** | Find area of a rectangle by tiling and show it equals multiplying side lengths. |
| **NY-3.MD.7b** | Multiply side lengths to find areas of rectangles; represent products as rectangular areas. |
| **NY-3.MD.7c** | Use tiling to show area of a rectangle with side lengths `a` and `b + c` is `a×b + a×c`; represent distributive property. |
| **NY-3.MD.7d** | Recognize area as additive; find areas of figures composed of non-overlapping rectangles. |
| **NY-3.MD.8a** | Solve real-world and mathematical problems involving perimeters of polygons; find perimeter given side lengths or one unknown side given perimeter and other sides. |
| **NY-3.MD.8b** | Identify rectangles with the same perimeter and different areas, or same area and different perimeters. |

### Geometry (NY-3.G)

| Code | Standard |
|------|----------|
| **NY-3.G.1** | Recognize and classify polygons based on number of sides/vertices (triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, hexagons); identify shapes that do not belong to a given subcategory. |
| **NY-3.G.2** | Partition shapes into parts with equal areas; express the area of each part as a unit fraction of the whole. |

### Key Grade 3 Math Topics / Module Sequence (Eureka/EngageNY)

The EngageNY/Eureka Math Grade 3 module sequence commonly used in NYC:

1. **Module 1:** Properties of Multiplication and Division and Solving Problems with Units of 2–5 and 10
2. **Module 2:** Place Value and Problem Solving with Units of Measure
3. **Module 3:** Multiplication and Division with Units of 0, 1, 6–9, and Multiples of 10
4. **Module 4:** Multiplication and Area
5. **Module 5:** Fractions as Numbers on the Number Line
6. **Module 6:** Collecting and Displaying Data
7. **Module 7:** Geometry and Measurement Word Problems

**Required fluencies for Grade 3:**
- Multiply and divide within 100 (NY-3.OA.7).
- Add and subtract within 1,000 (NY-3.NBT.2).

## 3. Standards Codes Used

- **NYS Next Generation ELA codes:** `3R1–3R9`, `3RF3`, `3RF4`, `3W1–3W7`, `3SL1–3SL6`, `3L3–3L6`, plus banded `3-5L1` and `3-5L2`.
  - These replace the older Common Core codes such as `3.RL.1`, `3.RI.1`, `3.W.1`, etc.
- **NYS Next Generation Mathematics codes:** `NY-3.OA.1–NY-3.OA.9`, `NY-3.NBT.1–NY-3.NBT.4b`, `NY-3.NF.1–NY-3.NF.3d`, `NY-3.MD.1–NY-3.MD.8b`, `NY-3.G.1–NY-3.G.2`.
- **Brooklyn/NYC-specific:** No separate borough standards exist; schools follow NYC DOE/NYSED policy and may use approved curricula such as those under **NYC Reads** (ELA) and **NYC Solves** (math, expanding into elementary).
