# NYC / Brooklyn Grade 4 Social Studies Curriculum Research

**Course title:** New York State and Local History and Government  
**Grade:** 4  
**Setting:** New York City Public Schools (applies to Brooklyn and all boroughs)  
**Primary curriculum:** NYC DOE *Passport to Social Studies*  
**State framework:** NYSED *New York State K-8 Social Studies Framework*  

Grade 4 Social Studies focuses on **New York State and local communities and their change over time**, integrating **history, geography, economics, and government/civics**. Teachers are encouraged to make local connections throughout the course; for Brooklyn students this means linking content to Kings County, New York City, and the surrounding region.

---

## 1. Major Units

The NYC DOE organizes the year into **five Passport to Social Studies units**.

| # | Unit Title | Approximate NYSED Key Ideas | Disciplines |
|---|-----------|----------------------------|-------------|
| 1 | **The Geography of New York State** | SS.4.1 | Geography |
| 2 | **Native Americans: First Inhabitants of New York State** | SS.4.2 | History, Geography, Civics |
| 3 | **Colonial and Revolutionary Periods** | SS.4.3 | History |
| 4 | **Freedom and the New Nation: Federal, State, and Local Government** | SS.4.4 (and SS.4.5 themes) | Civics / Government |
| 5 | **Making the Empire State: Immigration, Industrialization, and the Westward Movement** | SS.4.5, SS.4.6, SS.4.7 | History, Economics, Geography |

The state framework divides the same content into **seven Key Ideas** (SS.4.1–SS.4.7), which are the official NYSED content standards. NYC’s five units group those Key Ideas for pacing and instruction.

## 2. Key Topics, Vocabulary, and Skills

### Unit 1 / SS.4.1 — Geography of New York State
**Core idea:** New York State has a diverse geography that can be represented and examined with different maps.

- **Topics**
  - Physical vs. political vs. thematic maps
  - Major physical features: mountains, plateaus, rivers, lakes, Atlantic Ocean, Long Island Sound
  - Climate and vegetation regions
  - Political features: capital (Albany), five most populous cities, borders with neighboring states and Canada
  - Scale, directionality, latitude/longitude
- **Vocabulary**
  - physical map, political map, thematic map, plateau, river, lake, climate, vegetation, latitude, longitude, border, capital, population
- **Skills**
  - Create a political map of NYS including Albany, the five largest cities, and the student’s own community
  - Use maps of different scales to locate NYS within the U.S. and the world
  - Compare geographic features of the NYC region with another region

### Unit 2 / SS.4.2 — Native American Groups and the Environment
**Core idea:** The Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) and Algonquian-speaking groups were the first inhabitants of the region that became New York State.

- **Topics**
  - Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) and Algonquian peoples: Mahicans, Shinnecock, Lenni Lenape, Munsees
  - How geography influenced settlement locations
  - Adaptation to and modification of the environment for food, clothing, shelter
  - Matrilineal clan structure, decision-making, governance, and oral tradition
  - Roles of men, women, and children; technology and transportation
  - Native American contributions visible today
- **Vocabulary**
  - Haudenosaunee, Iroquois, Algonquian, clan, matrilineal, Longhouse, adaptation, environment, natural resources, oral history, artifact
- **Skills**
  - Compare/contrast patterns of organization and governance of Native American groups
  - Examine how cultures used natural resources to meet needs and wants
  - Analyze images, maps, and artifacts as historical evidence

### Unit 3 / SS.4.3 — Colonial and Revolutionary Period in New York
**Core idea:** European exploration led to colonization; colonial New York was home to many peoples and played an important role in the American Revolution.

- **Topics**
  - European explorers of New York waterways: Giovanni da Verrazano, Henry Hudson, Samuel de Champlain
  - Dutch colony of New Netherland; transition to English colony of New York
  - Interactions among Native Americans, Dutch/French fur traders, missionaries, settlers
  - Diverse colonial population: Europeans, free and enslaved Africans
  - French and Indian War; major battles in New York
  - Causes of the American Revolution: political and economic rights
  - New York’s strategic role; Loyalists, Patriots, Native American alliances, African American volunteers
  - Battles of Long Island and Saratoga as turning points
- **Vocabulary**
  - exploration, colonization, New Netherland, colony, trading post, fur trade, missionary, Dutch, English/British, enslaved, free African, French and Indian War, alliance, American Revolution, Loyalist, Patriot, turning point, Battle of Long Island, Battle of Saratoga
- **Skills**
  - Map explorer voyages and identify which Native peoples they encountered
  - Compare life under Dutch and British rule, especially for African Americans
  - Identify multiple perspectives on land ownership and resource use
  - Explain causes and effects of the Revolution using evidence

### Unit 4 / SS.4.4 — Government: Federal, State, and Local
**Core idea:** There are different levels of government in the United States and New York State; government protects rights and promotes the common good.

- **Topics**
  - Basic structure of the federal government (President, Congress, courts)
  - New York State Constitution and the three branches of state government
  - New York State Seal and flag symbols
  - How a bill becomes a law in New York State
  - Local government: counties, cities, towns, villages; NYC boroughs and counties
  - Rights and freedoms guaranteed by the U.S. Constitution, NYS Constitution, and state laws
  - Responsibilities of citizenship: obeying laws, voting, jury duty, active citizenship
- **Vocabulary**
  - federal government, state government, local government, legislative, executive, judicial, governor, senator, assemblyperson, mayor, bill, law, constitution, rights, freedoms, responsibilities, citizen, county, borough, jury, vote
- **Brooklyn-specific application**
  - Brooklyn is one of the five boroughs of New York City and is coterminous with **Kings County**.
  - Students identify the borough they live in and the county it is located in.
  - Students identify their local elected leaders (city council member, borough president, state senator, assemblymember).
- **Skills**
  - Use graphic organizers to show branches and levels of government
  - Trace the steps for a bill to become a NYS law
  - Distinguish rights from responsibilities

### Unit 5 / SS.4.5, SS.4.6, SS.4.7 — Freedom, Industrialization, Immigration, and Migration
**Core idea:** Different groups fought for rights and freedoms; New York’s economy, transportation, and population changed dramatically from the 1800s to the present.

- **Topics**
  - Slavery in New York and the abolition movement (Samuel Cornish, Frederick Douglass, William Lloyd Garrison, Harriet Tubman)
  - Women’s rights movement: Amelia Bloomer, Sojourner Truth, Elizabeth Cady Stanton, Lucretia Mott, Susan B. Anthony, Matilda Joslyn Gage, Elizabeth Blackwell; Seneca Falls convention
  - New York State’s role in the Civil War; local community contributions
  - Westward movement, Erie Canal, Native American trails, corduroy roads, turnpikes
  - Transportation and communication innovations: steam engine, telegraph, railroads
  - Economic activities: farming, mining, lumbering, finance/banking, manufacturing, service and high-tech industries
  - Entrepreneurs and inventors associated with NYS
  - Immigration through Ellis Island and the Statue of Liberty; immigrant experiences, factory conditions, sweatshops, Triangle Shirtwaist Fire, child labor, labor unions
  - Great Migration of African Americans to New York City; Harlem Renaissance
- **Vocabulary**
  - abolition, slavery, Underground Railroad, suffrage, Seneca Falls, Civil War, Union, Erie Canal, canal, turnpike, corduroy road, steam engine, telegraph, industrialization, manufacturing, entrepreneur, inventor, finance, banking, agriculture, mining, lumbering, service industry, high-technology industry, immigration, Ellis Island, Statue of Liberty, sweatshop, labor union, child labor, naturalization, Great Migration, Harlem Renaissance
- **Skills**
  - Research a local community’s Civil War contribution
  - Trace changes in local economic activities over the last 50 years
  - Create maps showing economic activities across NYS regions
  - Compare immigrant/migrant experiences and explain push/pull factors

## 3. NYC/NYSED Social Studies Framework Codes

### NYSED Learning Standards for Social Studies (1996)
The Key Ideas reference five broad learning standards:

| Code | Standard |
|------|----------|
| **1** | History of the United States and New York |
| **2** | World History |
| **3** | Geography |
| **4** | Economics |
| **5** | Civics, Citizenship, and Government |

### Grade 4 Key Ideas (NYSED K-8 Framework)
Official NYSED codes use the prefix **SS.4.x**:

| Code | Key Idea | NYS Standards | Unifying Themes |
|------|----------|---------------|-----------------|
| **SS.4.1** | Geography of New York State | 3 | GEO |
| **SS.4.2** | Native American Groups and the Environment | 1, 3, 5 | ID, MOV, GEO, GOV |
| **SS.4.3** | Colonial and Revolutionary Period in New York | 1, 3, 4 | MOV, TCC, GEO, SOC, GOV |
| **SS.4.4** | Government | 1, 5 | GOV, CIV |
| **SS.4.5** | In Search of Freedom and a Call for Change | 1, 5 | ID, TCC, SOC, CIV |
| **SS.4.6** | Westward Movement and Industrialization | 1, 3, 4 | MOV, TCC, GEO, ECO, TECH |
| **SS.4.7** | Immigration and Migration from the Early 1800s to the Present | 1, 3, 4, 5 | ID, MOV, CIV, ECO, EXCH |

Each Key Idea breaks into **Conceptual Understandings** (e.g., **SS.4.3.a**, **SS.4.3.b**) and **Content Specifications** (e.g., **SS.4.3.d.1**).

### Unifying Themes (abbreviations used above)
- **ID** — Individual Development and Cultural Identity  
- **MOV** — Development, Movement, and Interaction of Cultures  
- **TCC** — Time, Continuity, and Change  
- **GEO** — Geography, Humans, and the Environment  
- **SOC** — Development and Transformation of Social Structures  
- **GOV** — Power, Authority, and Governance  
- **CIV** — Civic Ideals and Practices  
- **ECO** — Creation, Expansion, and Interaction of Economic Systems  
- **TECH** — Science, Technology, and Innovation  
- **EXCH** — Global Connections and Exchange  

### Grade 4 Social Studies Practices
The framework expects students to practice disciplinary skills organized in six strands. NYSED uses **SS.SSP.4**; some districts/websites abbreviate them as **4P.A–4P.F**.

| Strand | Focus |
|--------|-------|
| **A / 4P.A** | Gathering, Interpreting, and Using Evidence |
| **B / 4P.B** | Chronological Reasoning and Causation |
| **C / 4P.C** | Comparison and Contextualization |
| **D / 4P.D** | Geographic Reasoning |
| **E / 4P.E** | Economics and Economic Systems |
| **F / 4P.F** | Civic Participation |

Representative practice standards include:
- Develop questions about NYS history, geography, economics, and government
- Recognize and analyze primary/secondary sources (art, photographs, artifacts, maps, graphs)
- Identify authorship, purpose, format, and point of view
- Use timelines, B.C.E./C.E., decades, and centuries
- Identify causes and effects, long-term and immediate
- Compare regions of NYS; describe geography-history-economics relationships
- Use maps and models to describe location and human-environment interaction
- Explain scarcity, resources, specialization, trade, money, taxes, unemployment
- Demonstrate respect, participate in civic activities, identify rights and responsibilities

### Literacy in Social Studies
Grade 4 also applies Common Core / Next Generation Literacy Standards for reading, writing, speaking, and listening in history/social studies, including:
- Citing textual evidence from primary and secondary sources
- Summarizing sources and identifying point of view
- Integrating visual information with print/digital text
- Writing informative/explanatory and argument texts
- Conducting short research projects
