The Last Piece

Marcus had always loved puzzles. Every Saturday morning, he would spread the pieces across the kitchen table and lose himself for hours. But when his family moved from their apartment in the Bronx to a smaller place in Queens, one of his puzzle boxes got lost in the move. It was his favorite — a thousand-piece map of the world, with every country in a different color. He searched every box twice, but it was gone.

For weeks, Marcus sulked. He refused to start any new puzzle, certain that none would be as good. His grandmother, who had come to stay with them during the move, watched him quietly from her chair. One afternoon she set a brand-new box on the table in front of him. “Every puzzle has a last piece,” she said, “but you have to put down the first one to find it.” Marcus stared at the box for a long time. Then, slowly, he opened it.

The Garden on the Rooftop

Nobody paid attention to the roof of 47 Elmwood Avenue until the summer Yara decided to do something about it. The rooftop was flat and empty, just cracked tar paper baking in the July heat. Yara had seen a documentary about rooftop gardens and couldn’t stop thinking about it. She drew up a plan, knocked on every door in the building, and convinced twelve neighbors to pitch in for soil, seeds, and wooden planters.

By September, the roof was covered in tomatoes, peppers, herbs, and sunflowers taller than Yara herself. Neighbors who had lived in the building for years and barely knew each other’s names were sharing meals made from vegetables they had grown together. The rooftop wasn’t just a garden — it had become the heart of the whole building.
