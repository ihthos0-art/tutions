The Lost Library Book

Manha had borrowed a library book called Ocean Animals. She was so excited that she read three chapters the very first night. The book had glossy pictures of dolphins, giant whales, and deep-sea fish that glowed in the dark. She promised herself she would take very good care of it because it belonged to everyone at the library, not just her.

A week passed, and when Manha went to put the book back on her shelf, it was not there. She searched under her bed, in her backpack, and inside every drawer in her room. Her stomach began to feel tight and anxious. The book was due back in two days, and she had no idea where it had gone.

Manha told her mother what happened. Her mother said calmly, “Think carefully about the last place you had it.” Manha remembered reading it during lunch at school. The next morning, she went straight to her teacher and explained everything honestly. Her teacher walked her to the cafeteria lost and found. And there it was — Ocean Animals, sitting right on top of the pile, safe and unharmed.

Manha felt her whole body relax the moment she held the book again. She returned it to the library on time and even apologized to the librarian for almost losing it. The librarian smiled and said, “The best thing you did was tell the truth and keep looking.” Manha walked home feeling lighter than she had in days. She learned that being responsible did not just mean being careful — it also meant being honest when something went wrong.

The Science Fair

Javier had been working on his science fair project for three whole weeks. He was building a model volcano out of clay, and he had practiced making it erupt with baking soda and vinegar at least six times at home. Every eruption worked perfectly — a white foamy burst that shot straight up over the top of the cone. He was certain he had a strong chance at first place.

The night before the fair, disaster struck. While Javier was carrying the volcano to the car, he stumbled on the front step. The model hit the ground and split right down the middle. Javier stared at the two pieces in the driveway and felt tears pressing behind his eyes. Three weeks of careful work, ruined in one second. He wanted to just go to bed and forget the whole thing had ever happened.

His father sat down beside him on the driveway. “We can fix this,” he said quietly. Together they worked past midnight, pressing the clay back together and smoothing every crack. His father found brown paint in the garage and covered the damage. It was not as perfect as before, but it looked like a rough, real volcano — the kind you might see a photograph of in a science book.

At the science fair the next morning, Javier did not win first place. But when the eruption went off in a huge foamy burst, the entire gym cheered. His teacher gave him a special ribbon that said “Most Determined.” Javier taped it to his bedroom door that evening and decided it was his favorite prize of all — not because he had won, but because he had not given up when everything felt impossible.

The New Girl

On the first day of October, a new girl named Priya arrived in Ms. Chen’s third grade class. She had just moved from another city and did not know anyone. She sat in the back row and kept her eyes on her desk. When the other students laughed at something the teacher said, Priya just stared at her pencil case because she had missed the joke.

At lunch, Priya sat alone at the end of a long table. A girl named Sofia noticed and carried her tray over without saying anything. She just sat down next to Priya and started eating her sandwich. After a few quiet minutes, Sofia pointed at a mural on the cafeteria wall. “I helped paint that dolphin,” she said. Priya looked up for the first time all day. “You did?” she asked. That was all it took. They talked for the entire rest of lunch.

By Friday, Priya was saving Sofia a seat every morning before school. She still missed her old friends, but the ache was beginning to feel smaller. She had learned something important that week: sometimes a new friend does not give a big speech or do something grand. Sometimes they just sit down next to you when no one else does, and that is more than enough.
