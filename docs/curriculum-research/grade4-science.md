# NYC / Brooklyn Grade 4 Science Curriculum Research

Date: 2026-06-27
Scope: Grade 4 science for New York City Public Schools (including all Brooklyn districts). Brooklyn schools follow the citywide NYC DOE science scope and sequence; there is no separate Brooklyn-only curriculum.

## 1. Governing Standards

Grade 4 science in NYC is aligned to the **New York State P-12 Science Learning Standards (NYSSLS)**, which are based on / aligned with the **Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS)**. The standards use three dimensions:

- **Disciplinary Core Ideas (DCIs)** – the science content
- **Science and Engineering Practices** – how students investigate and design
- **Crosscutting Concepts** – patterns, cause and effect, energy and matter, systems and system models, stability and change

The official NYC instructional resource is the **Amplify Science** curriculum, with city-specific pacing and implementation guidance published by the NYC Department of Education.

## 2. Major Units

NYC Grade 4 science is organized into **four ~8-week units** in Amplify Science:

### Unit 1: Energy Conversions — *Blackout in Ergstown*
- Students investigate how electrical systems convert and transfer energy.
- They design, test, and refine devices that convert energy from one form to another.
- Real-world problem: making a town's electrical system more reliable.

### Unit 2: Vision and Light — *Investigating Animal Eyes*
- Students study how light, eye structures, and the brain work together so animals can see.
- They use models to explain why a population of Tokay geckos is declining in a Philippine rainforest.
- Combines physical science (light), life science (structures and information processing), and cause-and-effect reasoning.

### Unit 3: Earth's Features — *Mystery in Desert Rocks Canyon*
- Students read evidence from rock layers and fossils to infer how a landscape changed over time.
- They investigate weathering, erosion, and deposition by water, ice, wind, and vegetation.
- They use maps to describe patterns of Earth's features and argue about geologic history.

### Unit 4: Waves, Energy and Information — *Investigating How Dolphins Communicate*
- Students develop models of waves (amplitude, wavelength) and investigate how sound travels.
- They explore how dolphins send and receive signals and how humans encode/decode information over distances.

### Engineering Design (3-5 Band, Integrated)
Across all units, students apply the 3-5 engineering design standards:
- Define problems with criteria and constraints.
- Generate and compare solutions.
- Plan fair tests, control variables, and refine prototypes.

## 3. Key Vocabulary and Concepts

### Physical Science
- **Energy**, **energy transfer**, **energy conversion**
- **Speed**, **motion**, **collision**, **force**
- **Forms of energy**: sound, light, heat, electrical
- **Electric current**, **battery**, **circuit**
- **Renewable** and **nonrenewable resources**
- **Fossil fuels**, **natural resources**, **environmental impact**
- **Wave**, **amplitude**, **wavelength**, **pattern**
- **Digitized information**, **code**, **binary pattern**, **Morse code**

### Life Science
- **Structure and function** (internal and external structures)
- **Survival**, **growth**, **behavior**, **reproduction**
- **Sense receptor**, **brain**, **information processing**
- **Light reflection**, **vision**, **eye**

### Earth and Space Science
- **Rock formation**, **sedimentary rock**, **rock layers**
- **Fossil**, **evidence**, **relative time**
- **Weathering**, **erosion**, **deposition**
- **Water, ice, wind, vegetation** as agents of change
- **Topographic map**, **landform**, **pattern**
- **Natural hazards**: earthquake, flood, tsunami, volcanic eruption
- **Engineering solutions** to reduce hazard impacts

### Science & Engineering Practices
- Ask questions and define problems
- Develop and use models
- Plan and carry out investigations
- Analyze and interpret data
- Use mathematics and computational thinking
- Construct explanations and design solutions
- Engage in argument from evidence
- Obtain, evaluate, and communicate information

### Engineering Design Terms
- **Criteria**, **constraints**, **prototype**, **fair test**, **variable**, **model**, **evidence**, **claim**, **argument**, **explanation**

## 4. Standards Codes

### Performance Expectations by Unit

#### Unit 1: Energy Conversions
| Code | Performance Expectation |
|------|--------------------------|
| **4-PS3-1** | Use evidence to construct an explanation relating the speed of an object to the energy of that object. |
| **4-PS3-2** | Make observations to provide evidence that energy can be transferred by sound, light, heat, and electric currents. |
| **4-PS3-4*** | Apply scientific ideas to design, test, and refine a device that converts energy from one form to another. |
| **4-ESS3-1** | Obtain and combine information to describe that energy and fuels are derived from natural resources and their uses affect the environment. |

#### Unit 2: Vision and Light
| Code | Performance Expectation |
|------|--------------------------|
| **4-LS1-1** | Construct an argument that plants and animals have internal and external structures that function to support survival, growth, behavior, and reproduction. |
| **4-LS1-2** | Use a model to describe that animals receive different types of information through their senses, process the information in their brain, and respond to the information in different ways. |
| **4-PS4-2** | Develop a model to describe that light reflecting from objects and entering the eye allows objects to be seen. |

#### Unit 3: Earth's Features
| Code | Performance Expectation |
|------|--------------------------|
| **4-ESS1-1** | Identify evidence from patterns in rock formations and fossils in rock layers to support an explanation for changes in a landscape over time. |
| **4-ESS2-1** | Make observations and/or measurements to provide evidence of the effects of weathering or the rate of erosion by water, ice, wind, or vegetation. |
| **4-ESS2-2** | Analyze and interpret data from maps to describe patterns of Earth's features. |
| **4-ESS3-2*** | Generate and compare multiple solutions to reduce the impacts of natural Earth processes on humans. |

#### Unit 4: Waves, Energy and Information
| Code | Performance Expectation |
|------|--------------------------|
| **4-PS3-2** | Make observations to provide evidence that energy can be transferred by sound, light, heat, and electrical currents. |
| **4-PS3-3** | Ask questions and predict outcomes about the changes in energy that occur when objects collide. |
| **4-PS4-1** | Develop a model of waves to describe patterns in terms of amplitude and wavelength and that waves can cause objects to move. |
| **4-PS4-3*** | Generate and compare multiple solutions that use patterns to transfer information. |

#### Engineering Design (3-5 Band, Integrated)
| Code | Performance Expectation |
|------|--------------------------|
| **3-5-ETS1-1** | Define a simple design problem reflecting a need or want that includes specified criteria for success and constraints on materials, time, or cost. |
| **3-5-ETS1-2** | Generate and compare multiple possible solutions to a problem based on how well each is likely to meet the criteria and constraints. |
| **3-5-ETS1-3** | Plan and carry out fair tests in which variables are controlled and failure points are considered to identify aspects of a model or prototype that can be improved. |

*Asterisk (*) indicates a performance expectation that integrates science content with engineering.*

### NYSSLS / NYLearns Code Format
NYSED's NYLearns standards browser also presents these expectations in a state-specific format such as:
- `S.4.PS.4.2` → 4-PS4-2 (light and vision)
- `S.4.LS.1.1` → 4-LS1-1 (structure and function)
- `S.4.LS.1.2` → 4-LS1-2 (information processing)
- `S.4.ESS` → Earth's Systems domain

The official NYSSLS documents and Amplify alignment primarily use the NGSS-style `4-PS3-1`, `4-LS1-1`, `4-ESS1-1`, etc., codes.
