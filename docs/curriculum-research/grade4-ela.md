# NYC / Brooklyn Grade 4 English Language Arts (ELA) Curriculum

## Scope Note

Brooklyn public schools operate under the New York City Department of Education (NYCDOE), which is required to follow the **New York State Next Generation English Language Arts Learning Standards (2017)** issued by the New York State Education Department (NYSED). These standards are mandatory and shape all local curriculum, instruction, and assessment decisions.

In addition, since the 2024-25 school year NYC has implemented **NYC Reads**, a citywide literacy initiative that requires elementary schools to use one of three NYCDOE-approved, science-of-reading-aligned core curricula: **HMH Into Reading**, **Wit & Wisdom**, or **EL Education** (the successor to the EngageNY/Expeditionary Learning modules). The standards below are what every Grade 4 student must learn regardless of which approved program a school uses.

---

## 1. Reading

### 1.1 Literary and Informational Text (Standards 4R1–4R9)

By the end of Grade 4, students read and comprehend literary and informational texts at or above grade level, balancing fiction, drama, poetry, nonfiction, biographies, and articles.

| Code | Standard |
|------|----------|
| **4R1** | Locate and refer to relevant details and evidence when explaining what a text says explicitly/implicitly and make logical inferences. |
| **4R2** | Determine a theme or central idea of a text and explain how it is supported by key details; summarize a text. |
| **4R3** | **Literature:** Describe a character, setting, or event, drawing on specific details in the text. <br>**Informational:** Explain events, procedures, ideas, or concepts, including what happened and why, based on specific evidence from the text. |
| **4R4** | Determine the meaning of words, phrases, figurative language, academic, and content-specific words. |
| **4R5** | **Literature:** Identify and analyze structural elements using terms such as verse, rhythm, meter, characters, settings, dialogue, stage directions. <br>**Informational:** Identify the overall structure using terms such as sequence, comparison, cause/effect, and problem/solution. |
| **4R6** | **Literature:** Compare and contrast the point of view from which different stories are narrated, including first- vs. third-person. <br>**Informational:** Compare and contrast a primary and secondary source on the same event or topic. |
| **4R7** | Identify information presented visually, orally, or quantitatively (charts, graphs, diagrams, timelines, animations, illustrations) and explain how it contributes to understanding. |
| **4R8** | Explain how claims in a text are supported by relevant reasons and evidence. |
| **4R9** | Recognize genres and make connections to other texts, ideas, cultural perspectives, eras, personal events, and situations. |

### 1.2 Foundational Skills (Standards 4RF3–4RF4)

Print-concept and phonological-awareness standards are addressed in earlier grades; Grade 4 focuses on decoding automaticity and fluent reading.

| Code | Standard |
|------|----------|
| **4RF3** | Know and apply grade-level phonics and word analysis skills in decoding words. |
| **4RF3a** | Use combined knowledge of all letter-sound correspondences, syllabication patterns, and morphology (roots and affixes) to read accurately unfamiliar multisyllabic words in and out of context. |
| **4RF4** | Read grade-level text with sufficient accuracy and fluency to support comprehension. |
| **4RF4a** | Read grade-level text across genres orally with accuracy, appropriate rate, and expression on successive readings. |
| **4RF4b** | Use context to confirm or self-correct word recognition and understanding, rereading as necessary. |

### 1.3 Comprehension Strategies and Expectations

- **Close reading:** Reread to locate evidence, make inferences, and support claims with text details.
- **Summarizing:** Identify central ideas/themes and condense a text in their own words.
- **Text structure:** Recognize sequence, comparison, cause/effect, and problem/solution in nonfiction; verse, rhythm, meter, and stage directions in literature.
- **Synthesis:** Integrate information from print, visual, and digital sources.
- **Text complexity:** By the end of Grade 4, students independently read and comprehend grade-level literary and informational texts.

## 2. Writing

Grade 4 students write for multiple purposes (to entertain, explain, and persuade), using print and digital tools. They plan, revise, strengthen, and publish texts, and they distinguish between conversational and academic language.

### 2.1 Opinion / Argument Writing (4W1)

| Code | Standard |
|------|----------|
| **4W1** | Write an argument to support claim(s), using clear reasons and relevant evidence. |
| **4W1a** | Introduce a precise claim, supported by well-organized facts and details, and organize the reasons and evidence logically. |
| **4W1b** | Use precise language and content-specific vocabulary. |
| **4W1c** | Use transitional words and phrases to connect ideas within categories of information. |
| **4W1d** | Provide a concluding statement or section related to the argument presented. |

### 2.2 Informative / Explanatory Writing (4W2)

| Code | Standard |
|------|----------|
| **4W2** | Write informative/explanatory texts to explore a topic and convey ideas and information relevant to the subject. |
| **4W2a** | Introduce a topic clearly and organize related information in paragraphs and sections. |
| **4W2b** | Develop ideas on a topic with facts, definitions, concrete details, or other relevant information; include text features when useful for aiding comprehension. |
| **4W2c** | Use precise language and content-specific vocabulary. |
| **4W2d** | Use transitional words and phrases to connect ideas within categories of information. |
| **4W2e** | Provide a concluding statement or section related to the information or explanation presented. |

### 2.3 Narrative Writing (4W3)

| Code | Standard |
|------|----------|
| **4W3** | Write narratives to develop real or imagined experiences or events using effective technique, descriptive details, and clear event sequences. |
| **4W3a** | Establish a situation and introduce a narrator and/or characters. |
| **4W3b** | Use dialogue and description of actions, thoughts, and feelings to develop experiences and events or show the responses of characters to situations. |
| **4W3c** | Use transitional words and phrases to manage the sequence of events. |
| **4W3d** | Use concrete words and phrases and sensory details to convey experiences and events precisely. |
| **4W3e** | Provide a conclusion that follows from the narrated experiences or events. |

### 2.4 Production, Response, and Research Writing (4W4–4W7)

| Code | Standard |
|------|----------|
| **4W4** | Create a poem, story, play, artwork, or other response to a text, author, theme, or personal experience. |
| **4W5** | Draw evidence from literary or informational texts to respond and support analysis, reflection, and research by applying the Grade 4 Reading Standards. |
| **4W6** | Conduct research to answer questions, including self-generated questions, and to build knowledge through investigating multiple aspects of a topic. |
| **4W7** | Recall relevant information from experiences or gather relevant information from multiple sources; take notes and categorize information, and provide a list of sources. |

## 3. Speaking and Listening

### 3.1 Comprehension and Collaboration (4SL1–4SL3)

| Code | Standard |
|------|----------|
| **4SL1** | Engage effectively in a range of collaborative discussions with diverse partners, expressing ideas clearly and building on those of others. |
| **4SL1a** | Come to discussions prepared, having read or studied required material; draw on that preparation and other information known about the topic. |
| **4SL1b** | Follow agreed-upon norms for discussions and carry out assigned roles. |
| **4SL1c** | Pose and respond to specific questions to clarify or follow up on information, and make comments that contribute to the discussion and link to the remarks of others. |
| **4SL1d** | Review the relevant ideas expressed and explain their own ideas and understanding of the discussion. |
| **4SL2** | Paraphrase portions of information presented in diverse formats (visual, quantitative, and oral). |
| **4SL3** | Identify and evaluate the reasons and evidence a speaker provides to support particular points. |

### 3.2 Presentation of Knowledge and Ideas (4SL4–4SL6)

| Code | Standard |
|------|----------|
| **4SL4** | Report on a topic or text, tell a story, or recount an experience with appropriate facts and relevant, descriptive details, speaking clearly at an understandable pace and volume appropriate for audience. |
| **4SL5** | Include digital media and/or visual displays in presentations to emphasize central ideas or themes. |
| **4SL6** | Distinguish between contexts that call for formal English versus informal discourse; use formal English when appropriate to task and situation. |

## 4. Vocabulary and Language Conventions

### 4.1 Vocabulary Acquisition and Use (4L4–4L6)

| Code | Standard |
|------|----------|
| **4L4** | Determine or clarify the meaning of unknown and multiple-meaning words and phrases, choosing flexibly from a range of strategies. |
| **4L4a** | Use context (definitions, examples, or restatements in text) as a clue to the meaning of a word or phrase. |
| **4L4b** | Use common, grade-appropriate Greek and Latin affixes and roots as clues to the meaning of a word (e.g., *telegraph, photograph, autograph*). |
| **4L4c** | Consult reference materials (dictionaries, glossaries, thesauruses) to find pronunciation and determine or clarify precise meaning. |
| **4L5** | Demonstrate understanding of figurative language, word relationships, and nuances in word meanings. |
| **4L5a** | Explain the meaning of simple similes and metaphors in context. |
| **4L5b** | Recognize and explain the meaning of common idioms, adages, and proverbs. |
| **4L5c** | Demonstrate understanding of words by relating them to their antonyms and synonyms. |
| **4L6** | Acquire and accurately use general academic and content-specific words and phrases, including those that signal precise actions, emotions, or states of being and that are basic to a particular topic. |

### 4.2 Knowledge of Language (4L3)

| Code | Standard |
|------|----------|
| **4L3** | Use knowledge of language and its conventions when writing, speaking, reading, or listening. |
| **4L3a** | Choose words and phrases to convey ideas precisely. |
| **4L3b** | Choose punctuation for effect. |
| **4L3c** | Distinguish between contexts that call for formal English (e.g., presenting ideas) and situations where informal discourse is appropriate (e.g., small group discussion). |

### 4.3 Conventions of Academic English (Grades 3–5 Band, L1 and L2)

NYSED organizes Language Standards 1 and 2 across the **3–5 grade band**; students are expected to know and use these skills by the end of 5th grade. Fourth grade instruction builds mastery toward these band goals.

**Core Conventions Skills (3–5L1)**

- Produce simple, compound, and complex sentences.
- Explain the function of nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs in general and in particular sentences.
- Use relative pronouns (*who, whose, whom, which, that*) and relative adverbs (*where, when, why*).
- Explain the function of conjunctions, prepositions, and interjections.
- Form and use regular and irregular plural nouns; use abstract nouns.
- Form and use regular and irregular verbs; form and use simple, progressive, and perfect verb tenses.
- Use verb tense to convey times, sequences, states, and conditions; recognize and correct inappropriate shifts in verb tense.
- Ensure subject-verb and pronoun-antecedent agreement.
- Use coordinating and subordinating conjunctions; use and identify prepositional phrases.
- Produce complete sentences, recognizing and correcting fragments and run-ons.
- Correctly use frequently confused words (e.g., *to, too, two; there, their*).

**Core Punctuation and Spelling Skills (3–5L2)**

- Capitalize appropriate words in titles; use correct capitalization overall.
- Use commas in addresses; use commas and quotation marks in dialogue/direct speech and quotations from text.
- Use a comma before a coordinating conjunction in a compound sentence.
- Use a comma to separate an introductory element from the rest of the sentence.
- Use punctuation to separate items in a series; form and use possessives.
- Use conventional spelling for high-frequency and studied words; add suffixes to base words (e.g., *sitting, smiled, cries, happiness*).
- Apply spelling patterns, rules, and generalizations (word families, position-based spellings, syllable patterns, ending rules, meaningful word parts).
- Spell grade-appropriate words correctly, consulting references as needed.
- Use quotation marks or italics to indicate titles of works.

## 5. Typical Grade 4 Topics in NYC-Approved Curricula

The following topic arcs come from the NYSED-authorized **EL Education / EngageNY Grade 4 ELA curriculum**, one of the three options schools may use under NYC Reads. These illustrate how the standards above are organized into content-based units.

| Module | Theme / Topic | Literacy Focus |
|--------|---------------|----------------|
| **1A** | Becoming a Close Reader and Writing-to-Learn: Haudenosaunee oral tradition, symbolism, and community building. | Close reading, fluency, explanatory paragraph writing, peer presentation. |
| **1B** | Reading Closely and Writing to Learn About Poetry: Poetry, poets, and becoming writers. | Poetry analysis, summary writing, creative response. |
| **2A** | Researching to Build Knowledge and Teaching Others: Interdependent roles in Colonial America. | Informational reading, inference, summarizing, basic research, note-taking, synthesis. |
| **2B** | Research to Build Knowledge and Teach Others: Animal defense mechanisms. | Informative writing, narrative writing, science journal use, research. |
| **3A** | Considering Perspectives and Supporting Opinions: Simple machines, force, and motion. | Readers Theater, opinion writing, informational and literary text integration. |
| **3B** | Reading Closely and Writing to Learn: Perspectives on the American Revolution. | Multiple perspectives, primary/secondary sources, opinion/argument writing. |
| **4** | Susan B. Anthony, the Suffrage Movement, and the Importance of Voting. | Firsthand/secondhand accounts, historical fiction (*The Hope Chest*), argument and civic discussion. |
